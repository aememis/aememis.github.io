IEMA: Exploring Complex Data Spaces with Body-Motion Interaction

MPhil thesis · Music, Communication and Technology
University of Oslo · 2025

A cloud of sound samples, laid out in a room. You walk into it the way you'd walk into a forest, and you hear whatever is near you. As you wander, the cloud grows new sounds in the places you keep returning to.

High-dimensional audio

A sound corpus is a high-dimensional data space. Every sample carries dozens of measurable qualities at once, and a screen can hold two or three of them at a time. IEMA moves the interface into a room. A body walking through space produces a continuous, high-bandwidth signal, and motion capture turns that into the query.

Audio signal processing

Each sample is analysed into a feature vector. Spectral and temporal descriptors come out through filtering and Fourier analysis, and those numbers decide where a sound sits relative to its neighbours. Distance in the room corresponds to distance in the audio features, so stepping toward a brighter region gives you brighter material. The geometry is built out of the sound itself.

Real time

Motion tracking, feature analysis, the evolutionary engine and the synthesis layer all run at once, and they all have to agree on the same moment. A slow path anywhere becomes latency you can feel in your feet. Getting the timing to hold was most of the work. What came out of it is open-source software with a live interactive audio tool at its centre.

The cloud evolves

Underneath is an interactive evolutionary algorithm. New sounds are bred from the ones you have been spending time near, and the regions you leave alone thin out. It has the shape of a recommender system: infer preference from behaviour, then serve more of it. Here the behaviour is where your body went. You express taste by walking around.

This was my MPhil thesis at the University of Oslo. It extends IEMI, an immersive motion-controlled installation published at the Sound and Music Computing Conference in 2024.
